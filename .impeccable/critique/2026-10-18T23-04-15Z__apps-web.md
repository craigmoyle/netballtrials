## Design Specificity Verdict

The interface is grounded in Chisholm’s logo, blue/plum palette, trial details, netball positions, and court-color controls. Its visual language is coherent with “The Sideline Clipboard,” but the page structures—event list, long registration form, and admin controls—remain fairly category-standard.

## Overall Impression

The app feels calm and trustworthy, especially when comparing event details. The biggest gap is eligibility confidence: a parent is asked to confirm eligibility without seeing the actual age cutoff. The registration form and operations page also take sustained scrolling and attention.

## What’s Working

- **Brand restraint:** The club logo, blue shell, plum actions, and restrained surfaces feel consistent without decorative clutter.
- **Event comparison:** Date, venue, arrival time, first whistle, fee, and status support the parent’s decision.
- **Form semantics:** Associated labels, native controls, autocomplete, section anchors, and a focused error summary are solid foundations.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Eligibility guidance omits the age rule

**Why it matters:** The page promises full criteria beside the confirmation, but shows membership and the six-game requirement without the event’s configured age range or the 31 December age calculation. Parents cannot confidently self-screen before entering details.

**Fix:** Show the exact age bounds and calculation beside the event summary and again near the eligibility checkbox. Keep the policy link as a reference, not the only source for a key eligibility rule.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`  
**Source:** `apps/web/src/routes/RegisterPage.tsx`

### [P2] “Continue to payment” can lead to an email handoff

**Why it matters:** With Stripe selected, the current public flow sends the parent to email for next steps after the button says “Continue to payment.” In fake mode it opens the in-page simulator. The same label describes different outcomes.

**Fix:** Use a neutral submit label, then explain the next step on the resulting screen (“Test payment simulation” or “Check your email for a secure link”).

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`  
**Source:** `apps/web/src/routes/RegisterPage.tsx`

### [P2] Registration is a long, unsequenced mobile form

**Why it matters:** The form has 12 fields across four sections in one scroll. At 390px wide, the page is about 2,486px tall, with no progress indicator. Entered data is held in component state, so leaving or reloading can lose it.

**Fix:** Consider a short guided sequence with visible progress and Back controls, retaining values between steps. Avoid browser persistence for children’s information without an explicit privacy decision.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`  
**Source:** `apps/web/src/routes/RegisterPage.tsx`

### [P2] High-impact admin actions compete with routine controls

**Why it matters:** Closing events/check-in and removing a PIN share primary-button treatment with routine settings. Confirmation dialogs help, but the consequences and reversibility are not apparent before opening them.

**Fix:** Separate lifecycle actions from routine settings and explain what closing/removing changes, including whether it can be undone.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`  
**Source:** `apps/web/src/admin/EventControls.tsx`

### [P2] Court-color setup makes operations unnecessarily tall

**Why it matters:** Each court has a separate pair of selects and save button stacked vertically. This becomes a long review-and-save sequence for four to six courts.

**Fix:** Use two columns on desktop and a single column on mobile, keeping each court’s saved state and action clearly scoped.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`  
**Source:** `apps/web/src/admin/EventCourtColors.tsx`, `apps/web/src/styles.css`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Sam — accessibility-dependent user:** Labels, native controls, and focused field-error summaries are positive. The long form is still a navigation burden; the simulator transition also does not move focus or announce the new heading.

## Minor Observations

- The logo links to the trial home, while “Chisholm Netball home” links to the club site; the two meanings of “home” may surprise users.
- The selection-policy link opens a new tab without indicating that.
- Desktop event navigation shows nine workflow links across three groups and wraps to a second line.
- The white team-color swatch is subtle on the pale background; its written name is an important backup.
